**PROBLEM STATEMENT · DIASPORA INFRASTRUCTURE**

**Closing the Trust Gap for Africans Building From Abroad**

*Working document · updated as user research comes in*

# **1. The Thesis**

Across Africa, diaspora members are among the most reliable sources of capital flowing into their home countries — not only into houses, but into shops, small clinics, boreholes, schools, family compounds, and other community or personal infrastructure. What they consistently lack is not the money, but a trustworthy way to direct it from a distance. Wherever verification is absent, funds get diverted, projects stall, and the sender is left with no way to tell the difference between a genuine delay and a quiet theft.

This is not a housing problem specifically. It is the diaspora trust gap — the same failure that shows up whenever someone abroad tries to build, fund, or maintain something back home through an agent, relative, or contractor they cannot personally supervise.

# **2. Problem Statement**

Diaspora Africans who want to raise infrastructure back home — a house, a shop, a family property, a small community asset — routinely lose money, time, and trust to a system with no independent verification layer. This shows up in four consistent ways:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **#** | **Problem** | **Impact** |
| A | Trust | Diaspora senders cannot verify that the person or team managing their project on the ground is legitimate, competent, or still working in their interest once funds are sent. |
| B | Fragmentation | Coordination happens across scattered, informal channels — phone calls, WhatsApp, bank transfers — with no single record either side can rely on. |
| C | Visibility | Once money is sent, the sender has no independent, real-time view of whether it was used as agreed, or whether progress reported is progress made. |
| D | Recourse | When funds are diverted, work stalls, or claims turn out false, the sender — often thousands of miles away — has no practical way to intervene or recover the loss. |

Why it matters: this friction doesn't just cause isolated losses — it suppresses investment altogether. Diaspora members who have been burned once, or who know someone who has, often stop sending money for anything beyond immediate family needs, even when they have both the will and the means to build. That is capital that never reaches the ground.

The gap: there is no trusted, verifiable, milestone-based way for someone abroad to fund a project back home and know — not hope — that their money is being used as agreed.

# **3. The Solution**

A verified, milestone-based platform that lets diaspora members fund infrastructure back home — a house, a shop, a family property, a community asset — without needing to personally supervise it. The platform is the trust layer between sender and builder, not a replacement for payments, logistics, or construction expertise.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Feature** | **What it does** | **Problem it solves** |
| Verified agent / contractor profiles | Every professional on the platform is identity-checked, credential-checked, and rated by past diaspora clients before they can take on a project. | Trust |
| Shared project workspace | A single space per project holding the agreed scope, budget, documents, and communication history, replacing scattered calls and WhatsApp threads. | Fragmentation |
| Milestone-based escrow | Funds are held on the platform and released only as each agreed stage is confirmed, not handed over in large, unsupervised chunks. | Trust, Recourse |
| Independent progress proof | Geo-tagged photos, video walkthroughs, or third-party inspection confirm real-world progress before funds move. | Visibility |
| Live status tracking | Both sender and agent see the same real-time view of budget spent, stage reached, and outstanding actions. | Visibility, Fragmentation |

Escrow is not the product — it is the mechanism. The product is the sender's confidence that money sent from thousands of miles away is doing exactly what it was sent to do.

# **4. Competitor Analysis**

The category is real and already validated by several live players, each solving a piece of the problem:

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Player** | **Model** | **Strength** | **Gap / whitespace** |
| DiasporaBuild | Marketplace connecting diaspora clients to vetted contractors, with an escrow wallet and daily project tracker. | Strong contractor vetting; deliberately scaling slowly to protect trust. | Construction-only; single vertical, not a general trust layer |
| House of Diaspora | Full-service, high-touch construction project management for the Kenyan diaspora. | Deep on-the-ground oversight and reporting. | Managed service, not self-serve software; single country |
| Propera | Connects diaspora investors to verified property developers, with trust scores and milestone payments. | Developer-side verification and trust scoring. | Built around institutional developers, not individual contractors or family builds |
| Escrow Africa | General-purpose escrow for real estate, construction, contracts, and vehicles, primarily in Ghana. | Simple, neutral fund-holding mechanism. | Generic tool, not tailored to diaspora workflows or milestone-specific construction |
| MyAwayHome | Earlier diaspora property management platform (seed-stage, no longer active). | Validated early diaspora appetite for the category. | Did not survive — a signal on execution risk, not market size |

Whitespace: most existing players are locked to a single country, a single project type, or a high-touch managed-service model that is hard to scale. None currently position themselves as a general-purpose diaspora trust layer that spans multiple asset types (not just housing) while remaining self-serve and corridor-focused. That combination — narrow enough to earn trust fast, broad enough to grow beyond one asset class — is the opening.

# **5. Go-to-Market Strategy**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Phase** | **Focus** |
| Beachhead | One diaspora corridor and one project type (e.g. UK/US-based Nigerian diaspora funding residential builds in a single city or region), so trust and vetting quality can be tightly controlled before expanding. |
| Trust seeding | Hand-vet a small first cohort of agents/contractors directly, rather than opening the marketplace broadly — early trust is the product, and it is easier to protect with a small pool. |
| Distribution | Reach diaspora communities where they already gather: WhatsApp and Facebook diaspora groups, church and hometown associations, and diaspora-focused financial or remittance platforms via referral partnerships. |
| Proof loop | Every completed milestone becomes a visible case study (before/after photos, on-time delivery record) that markets the next customer — turning delivered trust into acquisition content. |
| Expansion | Once a corridor is trusted, extend to adjacent project types (shops, community assets) before extending to new countries — depth before breadth. |

# **7. The Opportunity**

The research validates the whitepaper's core thesis directly: this is not a housing-specific problem, it is a trust-infrastructure problem that happens to be most visible in property because property is where diaspora capital is largest and most exposed. A platform that verifies agents, secures funds in milestone-based escrow, and gives senders real, independent visibility into progress closes the exact gap respondents describe — regardless of whether what they're building is a house, a shop, or a community asset.

Building this as infrastructure — not a single-purpose house-building app — also widens the addressable use case without diluting the core mechanism: verified people, milestone-locked funds, independent proof of progress.
